# Circle demo content

Use only fictional accounts and fictional care situations. The posts below are
written to make the feed feel varied without implying that they came from real
caregivers.

## Account one

Setup:

* Condition: Alzheimer’s disease
* Stage: Middle stage (moderate)

Post under **Alzheimer’s disease · Middle stage**:

> We tried putting tomorrow’s clothes in order on a chair before bed. Mornings
> have felt much calmer this week, and there have been fewer arguments about
> getting dressed.

## Account two

Setup:

* Condition: Vascular dementia
* Stage: Early stage (mild)

Post under **Daily care and routines**:

> A large calendar beside the kitchen table has helped us keep appointments
> visible. I write only one or two things per day so it does not feel
> overwhelming. What other simple visual reminders have worked for your family?

Response to account one:

> We do something similar with breakfast choices. Offering two clear options
> instead of asking an open question has made our mornings easier.

## Account three

Setup:

* Condition: Lewy body dementia
* Stage: Middle stage

Post under **Behavior and communication**:

> When my partner sees something that I cannot see, correcting him usually
> makes the fear worse. I have started acknowledging that it feels real to him,
> checking that he feels safe, and gently moving us to a brighter room.

Response to account two:

> A small whiteboard has helped us. We keep the date, the next meal, and one
> planned activity on it. More than that becomes distracting.

## Account four

Setup:

* Condition: Frontotemporal dementia
* Stage: Early stage

Post under **Caregiver wellbeing**:

> I finally asked my sister to cover one Saturday morning each month. It is not
> a lot of time, but having one dependable break on the calendar has made the
> week feel more manageable.

Response to account three:

> This is a helpful reminder. Meeting the emotion first has worked better for
> us than trying to prove what is or is not happening.

## Account five

Setup:

* Condition: Parkinson’s disease dementia
* Stage: Middle stage (moderate)

Post under **Family and support**:

> Our family group chat became a stream of vague offers to help, so I started
> sharing three specific tasks each Sunday. People are much more likely to pick
> one when the time and responsibility are clear.

Response to account four:

> A predictable break matters so much. I put mine on the shared calendar so it
> is treated like a real commitment, not optional free time.

## Recommended demo feed

Create the accounts and posts in this order:

1. Account five
2. Account four
3. Account three
4. Account two
5. Account one

Account one will then appear at the top of the feed. Use the other accounts to
add the suggested responses and add a few heart reactions across the feed.

Do not describe these as customer posts. In the recording, call them fictional
demo accounts or simply show the interaction without making a claim about who
wrote them.
